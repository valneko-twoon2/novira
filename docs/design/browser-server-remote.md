# Phones away from home (the remote listener)

The browser server ([browser-server.md](browser-server.md)) can open a third listener for this computer's own phones away from home, over Tailscale: on this computer's tailnet address, with the certificate Tailscale makes for its MagicDNS name, so a phone's browser trusts it with nothing installed. A phone gets in only with a passkey it made once, after pairing at this computer the way a phone pairs at home ([browser-server-pairing.md](browser-server-pairing.md)), and then signs in with its face, finger or code from anywhere on the tailnet. The desktop app runs it inside itself as it runs the home network ([browser-server-app.md](browser-server-app.md)). It is off until this computer's owner turns it on.

## Behaviour

- **On and off.** `novira-server` and the app enable it at every start (`Server::enable_remote` / `Embedded::enable_remote` with a `RemoteControl` over the data folder and `tailnet_source`). Whether it is on, the passkeys and a log are kept in `<data>/remote.json` (`backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/store.rs`: written whole through a temporary name; a file or an entry that is not well formed is dropped, so a damaged file lets fewer phones in, never more; at most `MAX_PASSKEYS` passkeys and `MAX_LOG` lines). While it is on, it opens at the start and at every half-minute look (`remote::maintain`, called by `lan::watch`), which also closes it when Tailscale stops or this computer's tailnet address or name changes (the next look opens it again), asks Tailscale for a fresh certificate once a day (`CERT_REFRESH`; swapped in place, `CertSlot`), and turns it off when nothing enrolled or signed in for `UNUSED_DAYS` since it was turned on. One look runs at a time. Turning it off closes it; the passkeys stay. Its port is kept in `server-remote-port`.
- **Tailscale** (`backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/tailnet.rs`). Only `%ProgramFiles%\Tailscale\tailscale.exe` is run (never one on `PATH`), directly, without a window, with fixed arguments and a timeout. `tailscale status --json` must say `Running`, give a tailnet IPv4 address (`100.64.0.0/10`) and a MagicDNS name (`host.tailnet.ts.net`, checked by `is_tailnet_name`) that is among its `CertDomains`; otherwise the problem is `tailscale_missing`, `tailscale_stopped` or `https_disabled` (MagicDNS or HTTPS certificates off in the tailnet's admin console). `tailscale cert` writes the certificate and key into a new folder under `<data>/remote/`, which is removed once read; the key is kept in memory only.
- **The listener** binds the tailnet address on its own port (`Listener::Remote`): TLS 1.2 and 1.3, HTTP/1.1, the same accept loop, header timeout and connection cap as the home network's. It closes as a unit: the port, its connections and event sockets, its sessions, codes and challenges.
- **Enrolling a phone.** This computer's page asks `POST /remote/code` for a pairing code in `https://<name>:<port>/remote#<code>` and its QR code (the remote listener's own `Pairing`, apart from the home network's). The phone, on the tailnet, opens it; the page (`backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/page.html`, `page.js`, its strings in `strings.json`, ja and en) asks to pair with `/pair/request` and `/pair/status` on this listener, shows the six-digit number, and once this computer allows it (`/remote/approve`) holds a session that may make one passkey. On a press (Safari asks for one) it gets `POST /remote/enrol/begin` (`{challenge, rpId, userId, userName, timeout}`), calls `navigator.credentials.create` (ES256 only, a discoverable credential, user verification required, no attestation) and posts the result to `POST /remote/enrol/finish` (`{id, clientDataJSON, authenticatorData, publicKey, algorithm}`, base64url). A passkey that checks out (`check_new` in `backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/passkey.rs`) is kept under the pairing's name; the session goes on and may make no other. One that does not check out is refused and the session may try again.
- **Signing in.** A phone without a session that opens `/` is sent to `/remote` (303), which offers to sign in: `POST /remote/sign-in/begin` gives `{challenge, rpId, timeout}`, `navigator.credentials.get` (user verification required, no credential named, so the phone offers its own), and `POST /remote/sign-in/finish` (`{id, clientDataJSON, authenticatorData, signature}`). A kept passkey that answers that address's challenge (`check_sign_in`) gives a session bound to the phone's tailnet address (`Pairing::grant`; 30 minutes without a request, 4 hours at most, as at home) and the page opens the app. Its last use and counter are saved, and every enrolment, sign-in and refused sign-in of a kept passkey is logged with the time, the name and the address.
- **Passkey checks** (`passkey.rs`): the client data's type, challenge (base64url, unpadded), origin `https://<name>:<port>` (no `crossOrigin`, no `topOrigin`); the authenticator data's relying party hash (the tailnet name), user present and user verified; for a new passkey the credential id in the attested data, and a public key that is P-256 SPKI and the one the attested COSE key carries; for a sign-in the ECDSA signature over the authenticator data and the client data's hash, and a counter that moves on when one was counting (synced passkeys keep zero). A challenge is 32 random bytes, good for `CHALLENGE_TTL`, once, for the address and purpose it was made for (a sign-in, or making a passkey in one session); an address may ask for `CEREMONIES_PER_WINDOW` a minute.
- **This computer's screen** (`frontend/src/app/RemotePanel.tsx`): when the server has the remote listener, the side bar's QR item (`HomeLinkButton.tsx`) has two tabs, the home network first (pressing the item still opens it) and away. The away tab has the switch, why it cannot open, the tailnet name, a button for an enrolment code (with the time left), the phones asking (name, address, number, allow, deny), the signed-in ones (disconnect), the passkeys with their last use (removing one takes a second press) and the five newest log lines. It reads `/remote/state` with the home network's state (`state/lan.ts`). The browser mock has a pretend one with `?lan&remote` (`&remote=on`, `&remote=missing`; `mockLan.ts`). The management routes are `/remote/state`, `/remote/enable`, `/remote/disable`, `/remote/code`, `/remote/approve`, `/remote/deny`, `/remote/revoke` (a session) and `/remote/forget` (a passkey: it cannot sign in again and its sessions end); in the app the commands `remote_state` … `remote_forget`.

## Boundaries

| Boundary | Guard |
|---|---|
| Who is answered | The remote listener answers only a peer in `100.64.0.0/10` that reached a local address in that range (and loopback from loopback); anything else is 403 before a credential is looked at. Only Tailscale's own interface has that address, so only devices of the tailnet reach it; which of them may is the tailnet's ACL's business, and then the passkey's. |
| `Host`, `Origin` | `Host` is exactly `<tailnet name>:<port>` of the open listener; the bare address, another name or port is 403. `Origin`, when sent, is `https://` that. A cross-site navigation may open only `/remote` (a scanned QR code). |
| Credentials | A device gets in only with a session: one from this computer's approval (which may make one passkey) or one from a passkey sign-in, from the address it was made for; the start token is no credential there. Public routes: `GET /remote`, `GET /remote.js`, `POST /pair/request`, `/pair/status`, `/remote/sign-in/begin`, `/remote/sign-in/finish`. Only a session that may still enrol reaches `/remote/enrol/*` (403 `not_enrolling`). |
| Passkeys | The relying party is the tailnet name and the origin this listener's; user verification is required both times; a challenge is single-use and bound to its address and purpose; a refused sign-in gets the same 403 `refused` whatever the reason. The store's public keys decide who gets in: a passkey enters it only through an approved enrolment. |
| Management | `/remote/state` and the others answer a loopback peer with the token only (403 for any device), as the pairing's do. The screen gets no key material. |
| What a device may run | The same as a phone at home: `LOOPBACK_ONLY` is refused (browser-server-lan.md). |
| Tailscale | One fixed program path, run directly with fixed arguments and the name it reported (checked), with a timeout; the certificate's key never stays on disk. |
| Bodies | `application/json`, at most 4 KiB on the passkey routes, base64url fields. |

Tests: `backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/passkey/tests.rs` (a pretend authenticator with a real P-256 key: each refusal of a new passkey and of a sign-in, the counter), `backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/store.rs` (the file, a damaged file, the caps, the log, the unused days), `backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/tailnet.rs` (the range, the names, the status, the PEM), `backend/crates/novira-server/src/remote/tests.rs` (through the router with a pretend Tailscale: on and off, the problems, who is answered, enrolling once, signing in, refusals, the address binding, forgetting, `LOOPBACK_ONLY`, the rate limit, the home network apart, the unused days, the daily certificate, a real TLS handshake for the name), the enrolment functions of `backend/crates/novira-server/src/pair/tests.rs`, `frontend/src/app/RemotePanel.test.tsx`, the remote calls of `frontend/src/backend/http/lan.test.ts` and `frontend/src/backend/tauri/lan.test.ts`, and the away tab in `frontend/e2e/lan.spec.ts`.

## Known limits

- Not checked against a real Tailscale and a real phone by the tests: they use a pretend `tailscale` and a pretend authenticator. Whether `tailscale cert` runs for a non-administrator Windows account depends on Tailscale's own rules.
- The certificate names this computer's MagicDNS name, so the name and the tailnet's name are in the public Certificate Transparency logs.
- Whoever controls the tailnet account can add a device that reaches the listener, and whoever controls the account a passkey syncs through (iCloud, Google) holds the passkey; both are needed. An unlocked phone in another's hands signs in with its code.
- Only ES256 passkeys; no attestation is read (the approval vouches for the phone). A counter of zero cannot tell a copy.
- Sessions live in memory: a restart, closing the listener or a change of the tailnet address makes a phone sign in again. A phone whose tailnet address changes signs in again.
- The phones' page state is per origin: the remote listener's (`https://<name>:<port>`) is not the home network's.
- The log keeps the newest `MAX_LOG` lines; an unknown passkey's refusals are not logged (there is no name to log).
