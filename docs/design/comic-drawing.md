# Comic panel pictures

How a panel of a comic gets its picture: the queue jobs the panels are drawn by, the words and reference pictures that keep a character the same, the regions of a panel with several characters, the poses, the earlier pictures a panel keeps, and a part of a picture drawn again. The saved document is in [comic-canvas.md](comic-canvas.md); the assistant's `scene` operation in [comic-scene.md](comic-scene.md).

## Behaviour

- A panel picture is an ordinary generation job (origin `generate`, destination `gallery`, one iteration; `state/comic/request.ts`). The frontend keeps the waiting queue item ids in memory (`pending` in `state/comic/store.ts`) and places the picture in the panel that asked for it (`results.ts`). The Draw button has three scopes (the selected panel, the page, the whole comic); the panels of the scope that have a prompt are queued in reading order, and queueing first waits for poses still being drawn and uploaded (`posesReady` in `state/comic/posePreset.ts`), so a panel never misses its pose picture.
- A project keeps a `cast` (up to 8 characters: name and look tags, 600 characters) and each panel the ids of the characters in it (`cast`); the words of a panel's characters are put in front of its prompt when it is drawn (`castTags` in `state/comic/request.ts`): the tags, then the parts (`parts`: hair, eyes, face, outfit, body, accessory, 120 characters each) that the framing of the panel's prompt shows (`state/comic/characterLook.ts`: close-up, upper body, from behind, else the whole person; a character marked `nonHuman`, such as a monster or an animal, gets every part in every framing and its reference picture is drawn with `no humans`), so a character's look is written once and a panel does not name what is out of frame; the portrait uses every part.
- Every panel is drawn with the comic's style words (`state/comic/style.ts`): quality words in front of its prompt and a shared negative behind its negative, the page's own or the comic's, else the main model family's defaults (`defaultStyle`). The drawing settings edit them (the user's artist tags go there), and so does the assistant's `style` operation. The defaults keep colour words out of the negative: panels are drawn in colour and a page is made black and white by its effect. The assistant is told how to word a panel prompt for the main model (`promptStyle` of `get_comic_page`: tags for the SD family, sentences for the newer families; `promptStyleOf`), as long sentences drew poorly on a tag model.
- A comic keeps places and a world line so the backgrounds stay the same (`places` and `world` of the document; `settingWords` in `state/comic/request.ts`): a panel that names its place (`place`) gets the place's words after its own prompt, and every panel gets the world's words last; the place's words to keep out join the panel's negative (`panelNegative`). They follow the prompt because a tag prompt names the people and what they do first. A close-up (`framingOf`) gets the place's words at `CLOSE_PLACE_WEIGHT`: at full strength they took the frame from the subject on the real engine. A panel drawn in regions keeps them in the whole prompt. The panel tools choose a panel's place, add a place and edit its words (`PanelPlace`). The assistant writes them with the `places` operation and the `place` of `panel`. A character's portrait and sheet views take no place: they are drawn on a plain background.
- A character may also hold a reference picture (`imageName`). The panels it is in are drawn with it as an IP-Adapter reference only when the comic turns its character references on (`castReference`, the strength, 0.1 to 1, set in the drawing settings or by the assistant's `castReference` operation; off by default): `characterRefs` shares the strength between the characters of a panel (`referenceWeight`), acts over the first 60 % of the steps (`CAST_REFERENCE_END`) with the plain adapter, and the CFG scale stays as set. When several panels are drawn and the panels use their characters' pictures (`usesCastPictures`: FLUX.2, or references on with an IP-Adapter installed) and a character has none, `ensurePortraits` (called by the Invoke build and `drawPanels`) first queues a picture of each such character alone (`portraitRequest`: its tags with a plain white background, 1024 square, no pose and no reference, so no place or other character rides along in the reference), waits for it and makes it the reference, then the panels are queued; if a picture never arrives (30 minutes) the panels are queued without it.
- A character may keep a sheet: pictures of it from three-quarter, the side, behind and of the face (`sheet`, `SHEET_VIEWS` in `state/comic/document.ts`), each drawn from its picture (the front) as the IP-Adapter reference with the view's words and the parts that view shows (`sheetRequest`), never from another view. `makeSheet` (`state/comic/generate.ts`) draws the front first when there is none, then queues the views the sheet lacks; a view already being drawn is not queued again. A view lands in its character when its picture comes (`bindSheetItem`, `sheetPending`; not saved, like `pending`), without an undo step, and undo leaves the views as they are. No screen shows the sheet now: `frontend/src/features/comic/CharacterSheet.tsx` is mounted only by the comic-wide mode of `CastCharacter` (no `panelId`), which no screen uses since the design tab was removed; the assistant's `sheet` operation makes it.
- With a FLUX.2 main model a panel gives its characters' pictures to the model itself as reference pictures (`castPictures` in `request.ts`, at most `MAX_REFERENCE_PICTURES`), instead of IP-Adapter references, and the guidance is not lowered; a sheet view gets the front picture that way. Qwen-Image-Edit-Plus is not used for panels: it needs a source picture.
- A panel that uses references takes as each character's reference the view its prompt needs (`viewFor` in `state/comic/characterSheet.ts`: from behind and close-ups by the framing, then side and three-quarter words), or the front when the sheet lacks that view or the gallery lost it.
- A comic character may be linked to a Characters library character (`libraryId`) that holds a LoRA (LoRA training lives in the separate app novira-train). A panel uses the LoRA of its only character that has one turned on, installed and of the main model's family (`castLora` in `request.ts`), its trigger word first in the prompt; with two or more such characters none.
- A panel with two or more characters placed on its pose's people (`poseCast`, set by the assistant's `who` or the cast tools; without it the human characters in the panel's order when they are as many as the people) is drawn in regions (`state/comic/castRegions.ts`): each character's words, and what that person does (`poseWords`, from a scene), become a regional layer over the grown box of its person, with `autoNegative`, and its reference picture gets the same mask as `regionImageUrl` (the same mask is stored once and reused); unplaced characters share the rest of the panel, and the panel prompt goes without the characters' words. The masks are intermediate gallery pictures drawn before the panel is queued. Only SD 1.5, SD 2 and SDXL main models (the model of the panel's own page); otherwise, and without placed people, a not-human character's reference is left out when a person shares the panel.
- A pose is sent as a ControlNet layer only when an OpenPose model fits the chosen main model; the assistant is told whether that holds (`poseControl` of `get_comic_page`). The layer says it is a pose (`controlType`), so a union ControlNet is told to read a body; left to guess, one drew the stick figures as wires. A person the assistant gives `back` is drawn from behind (the face hidden, the sides swapped), and `neck` places a framed figure (`readPoseSpec`). The poses are set in the panel at once, before their control picture is uploaded, so the balloons that follow can use where people stand. A panel's 3D rough adds its depth map as a second layer ([comic-rough.md](comic-rough.md)).
- A balloon the assistant gives a `speaker` (the person's number in the panel's pose) is put in free space near that person, away from the others and from the panel's other balloons, with its tail at their mouth (`state/comic/balloonPlace.ts`; the balloon's size is estimated from its words, and the screen fits the real one afterwards). A balloon the assistant makes or rewords is kept within `BALLOON_LIMIT` of its panel (about 0.42 across, 0.5 down and 0.11 of the area) by making its words smaller, and one with no free place at its size is made smaller until it fits (sound-effect lettering is left big). A panel also keeps `figures` (up to 8 boxes, in fractions of the panel) that the assistant writes for animals and other things a pose cannot show; balloons keep clear of them like of people, and they are not drawn or sent to the engine, so the prompt must put the animal there.
- A panel keeps `takes`: up to `TAKES_MAX` earlier pictures (a gallery name and the view it had), newest first. `setPanelImage` puts the picture a new one replaces (a redraw, a picture put in by hand) or that is taken out at the front; an earlier picture given again sits as it sat and leaves the list, which never holds the panel's own picture or one twice. Undo leaves the list as it is, like the pictures. The page toolbar's picture history (`frontend/src/features/comic/PanelTakes.tsx`) puts one back or drops it from the list (the picture stays in the gallery) and leaves out pictures the gallery no longer has; the assistant reads `earlierPictures` (the count) and puts one back with the `take` operation.

- A part of a drawn panel's picture can be drawn again (inpaint). The brush button of the page toolbar starts marking the selected panel (`state/comic/fixState.ts`, not saved): strokes over the panel, in fractions of it (at most `FIX_LIMITS`), with a brush width, the words for the part (empty: the panel prompt) and a strength (`FIX_STRENGTH`); selecting another panel ends it. The assistant marks boxes instead (the `fix` operation, up to 8 boxes in fractions of the panel). `redrawPart` (`state/comic/generate.ts`) draws the mask at the picture's own size through the inverse of how the picture sits in the panel (`panelToPicture` in `state/comic/panelFix.ts`: centre, turn, mirror, as `pageRender` draws it), stores it as an intermediate picture and queues `fixRequest` (`state/comic/request.ts`): origin `generate`, mode `inpaint`, the picture as the source, the panel's style, its characters' words and (when the comic uses them) reference pictures, no pose and no high-resolution pass, at the picture's size held to whole eights and 2048 pixels a side. The engine pastes the redrawn part back over the picture; the new picture lands in the panel with the old one's place, zoom and turn, and the old one goes to `takes`. A panel whose model is FLUX.2, a picture gone from the gallery or no model refuses at once (`fixTarget`), also for the assistant's `fix`.

## Known limits

- The words of the cast keep the hair and the clothes, not the face; the reference picture brings the face closer, and some difference between panels stays. With references off (the default) a face drifts more between panels unless the character has a LoRA.
- Whether a view drawn from the front picture turns as its words ask (above all the view from behind) depends on the model; a view that came out wrong is taken out and drawn again.

## Not implemented

- A panel job still waiting when the app closes lands in the gallery but not in its panel: the waiting ids are not saved.
